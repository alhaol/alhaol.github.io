The Barbell and the Forklift


A forklift can lift two tons. You cannot. Yet nobody walks into a gym and offers to bring the forklift over to the person straining under a heavy bar.

Everyone gets the joke. The point of the barbell was never to move the weight. It was to change the person lifting it.

I build AI systems that lift enormous cognitive loads, and I still do my hardest thinking with a pen on paper. Same reason the lifter waves off the forklift. A forklift moves a load with as little effort as possible, and modern AI is a brilliant forklift: it recalls across a million tokens and turns a plan into code while you watch. A barbell is the opposite. The weight is heavy on purpose, because the strain is what builds the muscle, and the muscle is a change inside you that no machine can install from the outside. Understanding works exactly like that.

The research keeps landing here. Students who take notes by hand beat laptop users on conceptual questions, because typing lets you transcribe word for word and skip the thinking, while writing forces you to compress it in your own words. The difficulty is not a flaw in learning. It is the mechanism.

Here is why AI makes this matter more, not less. When forklifts are everywhere, strength is what gets rare. Capture and recall are nearly free now, so the scarce thing is understanding, and paper is the cheapest place to earn it.

So I lift before I load. Sketch the design by hand, then hand the spec to the agents. Keep the forklift out of the one place strength is supposed to come from.
