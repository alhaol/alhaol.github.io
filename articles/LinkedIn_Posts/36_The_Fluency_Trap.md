Quick experiment. The next time AI hands you a perfect explanation of something you've wanted to understand for months, close the window and explain it back out loud. Most of us run dry after two sentences.

That gap has a name: the fluency illusion. When material feels familiar, the brain reports understanding. The feeling measures how smooth the input was, not what you stored. AI is the most efficient fluency machine ever built, so feeling knowledgeable has never been cheaper or less reliable.

The fix is old science. Roediger and Karpicke (2006): students tested on a passage retained roughly twice as much a week later as students who re-read it four times. The struggle to recall is not failed learning. It is the mechanism.

Applied to AI, it comes down to a verb. Don't ask the model to explain. Tell it: "I will explain this in my own words. Tell me what I got wrong and what I left out." The model stops being an oracle and becomes the tutor who knows exactly where your mental model breaks.

Teams fall for the same trap. A well-written incident report feels like learning from the failure. Have responders reconstruct the timeline from memory before opening the log; the divergence between their accounts is the real curriculum.

Explanation is input. Retrieval is learning. AI made the first one free. The second one is still on you.
