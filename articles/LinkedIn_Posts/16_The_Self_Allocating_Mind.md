The race to build bigger AI models is quietly ending. The next race is about something far more human: knowing how hard to think.

In May 2026, OpenAI shipped a setting that sounds minor and is anything but. Its workspace agents now let you choose a reasoning effort level, from none all the way up to extra high. Anthropic and Google are competing on the same axis. The frontier moved from how big the brain is to how well it spends its attention.

Here is why this matters. For years, every prompt got the same amount of thinking, whether it was trivial or profound. That wastes money on easy questions and fails silently on hard ones. Letting a model think longer on a hard problem often beats training a larger model in the first place. Effort, not size, became the cheaper path to better answers.

The deeper signal is what happens when the model sets that dial for itself. Knowing how hard to think is one of the defining traits of intelligence. A system that can judge a task's difficulty and then commit the right effort has the core of metacognition: reasoning about its own reasoning. That is the bridge toward general intelligence, and it is the same month a reasoning model used deep effort to crack an 80-year-old math problem.

For leaders, the move is clear. Stop buying one tier of intelligence. Match the depth of reasoning to the stakes of the task, budget compute like a portfolio, and ask every vendor a simple question: who sets the effort, you or the model?

The mind worth building is not the one that knows the most. It is the one that decides where to look.
