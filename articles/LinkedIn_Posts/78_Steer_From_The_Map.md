You ask three agents to draft the launch plan, step into a meeting, and come back to forty pages. You scroll to the bottom and hope.

That is not supervision. It is watching.

Adults read about 238 words a minute. One agent can burst about fifteen times faster, and working memory holds roughly four things at once. Follow the stream and all four slots fill with page numbers: the budget on page 2, the date on page 4, the promise on page 7. Nothing is left to judge the work. Decades of automation research show what happens to passive monitors: they lose the picture and recover slowest when something breaks.

So stop reading the stream. Make the agents draw a small map at every checkpoint: budget, timeline, scope and the customer promise; the few rules the plan cannot break; only the numbers that moved. Keep everything blue and the one problem amber, and your eyes find it without searching. When the promise drifts past scope, you fix one link in one sentence.

One catch: the map is the agent's account of its own work. Fill it from the finance model, real quotes and the actual documents, not from the agent's summary, and spot-check the raw output under one node now and then.

You will never read as fast as the machine writes. You do not have to.
