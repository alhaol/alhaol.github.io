Everyone keeps asking whether AI makes us dumber. Wrong question.

AI does not make you smarter or dumber on its own. It moves cognitive effort. The only thing that matters is where it moves it.

Aim AI at the answers you were supposed to produce yourself, and it quietly replaces you. Aim it at the work your brain is genuinely bad at, and it becomes an amplifier for the work only your brain can do.

I see this in two parts of my life. At work, no human holds a sprawling codebase or a tangle of product dependencies in working memory. So I let AI do the tracing: walk the dependency graph, flag the circular references, render a map I can actually look at. It removes the bandwidth tax of holding ten thousand details in my head, which frees the attention I need for the real decision. The map is the machine's. The judgment stays mine.

On a personal level, I am memorizing Quran using Tarteel, which listens as I recite and flags exactly where I slipped. That turns passive review into active recall, the single most powerful learning technique there is. AI handles the spacing and repetition I would abandon by hand, following the spaced-repetition science I first learned from Barbara Oakley's Learning How to Learn. The work of remembering stays mine. The bookkeeping goes to the machine.

One rule separates amplifying from atrophying: keep the retrieving, connecting, and deciding on your side of the keyboard. Hand the machine the tracing, the formatting, and the tireless repetition.

The same model that can answer so well you stop thinking can also question you until you think harder. The skill of this era is knowing which machine you are building every time you open the prompt.
