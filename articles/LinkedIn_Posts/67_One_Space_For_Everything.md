I forget a lot of things, and I think that is fine. What is not fine is the amount of information we have to consume.

Last week Google released EmbeddingGemma 2, and it changed how I think about that problem. It is a 740 million parameter model that runs on your own device, under the Apache 2.0 license.

Picture a folder of badly named videos. You record a voice memo asking for one moment, and the search brings back the clip, at the right minute. That works because the model turns voice, video, images and text into points on one shared map, where things that mean the same land close together. Search becomes "what sits nearest to my question?" No renaming, no tagging, no upload to someone else's cloud.

My recordings were never findable by their filenames. Now they can be findable by what is in them. One honest caveat from the model card: if you shrink the vectors to save space, text barely notices, but video and speech search lose up to a quarter of their quality. 256 dimensions is the safe default for mixed media.

The same map that lets me find a moment with my voice lets my agents find it with a sentence. Personal memory and agent memory can finally share one index.
