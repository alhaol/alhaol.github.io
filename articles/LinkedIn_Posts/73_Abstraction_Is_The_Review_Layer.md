In one 2025 survey, 40% of employees had received AI workslop in the past month. Each piece took almost two hours to deal with.

So people stop dealing with it. They skim, accept, forward. Work goes out under their name that nobody judged.

The research says this is not laziness. In five studies with 731 participants, people checked AI output when checking was cheap and skipped it when it was expensive. Reliance is a cost decision.

My own rule follows from that. When something is heavy and detailed, I do not read it. I ask the AI to abstract it first: a visual of the shape, the method it followed, and the three decisions that shaped the result, each with the option it rejected. Experience works at that level. I can judge it in minutes and change direction before anyone polishes the wrong thing.

One more piece helps: ask the agent to flag two or three places where it is least sure, with a reason. Not more. Among clinicians, each extra reminder per visit cut acceptance by 30%.

Ten minutes at the level of abstraction replaces the two hours nobody had.
