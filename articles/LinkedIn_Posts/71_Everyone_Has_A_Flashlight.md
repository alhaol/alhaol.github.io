Imagine everyone suddenly gets a powerful flashlight. You, your neighbours, and the burglar. It lights up whatever you point it at. It does not tell you where to point it.

That is PHP2Shell, the flaw that had governments taking their own websites offline this month. A researcher found it with a public AI model in about two weeks, aimed by twenty years of knowing where PHP's bugs hide. The same noise was heard in a 2024 audit, but nobody could prove it was a burglar, so it waited.

The flashlight is no longer the advantage, because attackers have it too. Knowing the house is. So is the authority to act before the proof arrives.

AI sets the pace. People set the direction.
