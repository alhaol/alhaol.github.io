20 new hires, all shipping working code with an AI assistant. Which 10 actually understand it?

You cannot tell from the code anymore. Working code used to be proof of understanding. With an assistant at hand, it is proof that the code runs.

Say 10 of the 20 understand data leakage. Without AI, 10 spot the leak in a pipeline and nine of those answers are earned. With AI, 18 spot it and still only nine are earned. Your dashboard sees 18. And the learning suffers too: developers who learned a new library with AI help scored 50% on a quiz about it, against 67% for those without.

This is a measurement problem, not a cheating problem. Let the assistant write the code. The knowledge that checks it, such as spotting leakage or judging whether a gain is bigger than the noise, has to stay with the engineer.

Open each new hire's first design review with 15 minutes of AI-asked questions, no assistant open, and keep the results out of performance reviews.
