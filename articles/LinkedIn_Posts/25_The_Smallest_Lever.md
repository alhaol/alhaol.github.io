A group of NYU researchers spent twenty years on a finding that should worry anyone with a vision board. Vividly imagining your success can make you less likely to achieve it.

The brain pays out the reward for the fantasy before any work happens, and the motivation to do the work drains away.

This is why real change rarely comes from ambition. It comes from small, boring habits that protect the three things a life runs on: attention, energy, and happiness. Sandeep Swadia (theMITmonk) lays out nine of them, from correcting a bad habit right after it fires, to shrinking an action down to two minutes, to forgiving yourself when a streak breaks.

The catch is the one every good intention hits: the day you need a habit most is the day you have the least energy to run it. That is a systems problem, and systems are what AI is good at. Point an assistant at the smallest repeatable action instead of the heroic plan. Let it triage your inbox, defend a recovery block, and show you last week instead of scolding you when you slip.

The goal was never the problem. Remove the friction around the next small step, and let the tool hold the structure so you can keep taking it.
