A coding agent can now produce ten working versions of almost anything in seconds. Not one of them can tell you which is worth keeping.

That single fact is reshaping where human value sits.

For most of computing history the expensive step was making the thing. Generative AI drove that cost close to zero. The step that did not get cheaper is choosing which output is actually good, and that is the work that is left. The name for choosing well under abundance is taste, and it does not ship with the tools that create the abundance.

Taste is not personal preference no one can argue with. It is judgment calibrated against thousands of real outcomes until it becomes fast and defensible. Andrej Karpathy, working alongside coding agents in 2026, said you still have to be in charge of aesthetics, judgment, and taste, and that AI code does not get better at this because there is no aesthetics reward in how the models are trained. Quality nobody scored cannot be optimized toward.

You cannot absorb taste by reading polished AI output either. Robert and Elizabeth Bjork showed that smooth, easy input creates a warm feeling of understanding that is largely an illusion. The judgment that lasts is built through the friction that fluent tools remove.

So the edge stops being what your team can build, since a competitor can clone that in a weekend of prompting. The edge becomes what your team can tell apart. Two organizations with identical tools diverge on the thousand small choices no one can outsource.

Point AI at generation, where it is extraordinary. Keep the evaluative seat human, where the leverage now lives.
