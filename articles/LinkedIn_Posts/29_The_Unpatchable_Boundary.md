Imagine hiring a brilliant new assistant who is fast, tireless, and a little too trusting.

You give them one rule: do what I ask. The trouble is they read every document you hand them in the same voice they hear your instructions. So when a vendor hides a line in the footer of an invoice that says "wire 5,000 to this account," the assistant just does it. They cannot tell your command apart from a sentence printed inside the paperwork.

That is prompt injection, and it is the biggest security problem with AI agents today. Most teams are waiting for a patch to fix it. The patch is not coming, because this is not a bug. It is how every AI model reads. Your instructions, the user's request, and any web page or document the agent opens all arrive as one stream of words, with no way to mark which words are trusted and which are just data.

In May 2026 Microsoft showed how far this goes. A hidden instruction buried in ordinary content tricked AI agents into running real code on the machine.

So you stop trusting the assistant to catch every trick and you change what they are allowed to hold at once. Meta calls it the Rule of Two. Never give one agent all three of these together: access to your private data, the habit of reading untrusted content, and the ability to send things out. Keep any two, drop the third, and the worst attacks lose their path. If a job truly needs all three, a human signs off before the agent acts.

Treat your AI agent like a new hire, not a trusted veteran. That one mental switch is most of the job.
