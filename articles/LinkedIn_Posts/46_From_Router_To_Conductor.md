Ask a router which model should answer a prompt and it gives you a fast, defensible answer. Ask an end-user which model they want and you have already lost them.

That gap is why model routing is a stepping stone, not a destination.

Routing picks one model per request to balance cost and quality. It is close to free money inside a pipeline you control. It gets awkward the moment a person is on the other end, because a person is not holding a cost-quality dial. They are holding a goal. Routing also fires once, at the front door, with no step that notices a weak answer and fixes it.

Orchestration is the layer that fits how people actually think. It keeps the goal in view, plans the steps, coordinates models and tools, and verifies the result before returning it. Routing does not disappear. It becomes one node inside that loop, wrapped by steps that can catch and repair its mistakes.

Think of it as the automatic transmission for models. The user states intent. The machine decides which gear, and when. The person on the other end should never have to know which model ran.

If your product asks a customer to pick a model or a quality tier, that is debt. Buy the routing, build the orchestration. The loop that turns a goal into a verified result is where your product is actually different.
