Most people treat every big decision the same way: get more information and wait for the answer to show up. For one kind of decision, that wait never ends.

Sort any decision on two questions. How bad is the damage if this goes wrong, and can it be undone? And after a fair amount of analysis, are the options still level?

That gives you four situations, not one. Low stakes with a clear winner is easy. Low stakes with level options is a coin flip, and deliberating over it costs more than the decision. High stakes with a clear winner is a big choice: laborious, but the work produces an answer. High stakes with options that stay level is a hard choice, and it is a different problem entirely. A big choice is hard because you do not yet know enough. A hard choice is hard because there is nothing left to know.

This is where AI changes the shape of your job. It is very good at the three quadrants that analysis can settle, so those get cheap. It cannot settle the fourth, because there is no fact there to find. The philosopher Ruth Chang calls those options "on a par": comparable, but not ranked.

So hard choices do not get easier. They just become a bigger share of what lands on your desk, and they arrive with a confident recommendation attached that they have not earned.

In that quadrant, stop asking the model what to do. Ask it to make the strongest case for each option and name what each one gives up. Then decide, and put your name on it.
