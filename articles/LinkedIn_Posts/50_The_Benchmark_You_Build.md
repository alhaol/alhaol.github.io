Every team that picks a model off a leaderboard is running an experiment somebody else designed, on a population that is not theirs.

The score is real. Its relevance to you is the part nobody checked.

Three forces pull a public number away from your reality. Contamination: Yang and colleagues showed that removing test items by matching word sequences fails against paraphrase, found 8 to 18 percent of HumanEval sitting in common pretraining corpora, and demonstrated a 13B model posting GPT-4 level scores after overfitting a rephrased test set. Reuse: Recht and colleagues rebuilt the ImageNet and CIFAR-10 test sets by the original recipe and watched published accuracy fall 11 to 14 percent, with nobody cheating. And incentives: a 2025 study of Chatbot Arena found undisclosed private testing and relative gains up to 112 percent from arena-specific data, which measures fit to the arena rather than quality.

Arabic teams hit this sooner, because the gap is visible in the language. Almost every Arabic benchmark is written in Modern Standard Arabic. Your users type Gulf or Levantine dialect and switch to English for technical terms. ArabicMMLU was built from real school exams with native speakers rather than translated from English, and AraDiCE added dialect and cultural coverage across Gulf, Egypt and the Levant. Both are worth running. Neither covers your dialect on your task.

So build that square yourself. Five hundred items sampled from real logs, not written from imagination. Labelled twice, with the disagreement rate recorded, because that rate is your ceiling. Half of it held private, never pasted into a hosted API. Then every claim, internal or vendor, gets restated as a score on your set.

Weights are a commodity that changes every few months. The evaluation set is the piece that appreciates.
