People spend about 47 percent of their waking hours thinking about something other than what they are doing. In the Harvard study that measured it, whether your mind was wandering predicted your mood more than twice as strongly as what you were actually doing.

The usual takeaway is "be present." That is a slogan, not a model, and it is wrong in a specific way. Learning from a mistake is attention on the past. Planning next quarter is attention on the future. Neither is waste.

A better split: attention is one account with three places to spend it, the past, the present and the future. Each has a productive form and a parasitic one that feels identical from the inside. Extraction takes the lesson and closes the file. Rumination runs the scene again and changes nothing. Planning ends in "when X happens, I will do Y." Worry generates the X and never attaches a Y.

One question sorts them, and it works on any thought. Does this change an action available to me right now? If yes, it is an investment. If no, the loop is just going to run again.

Now the part people skip when they write about AI here. These tools make the productive forms cheaper, and the wasteful ones cheaper by the same amount. A model will happily produce the hundredth retrospective of a conversation you already had, and the thousandth branch of a future you do not control. Worry with a research assistant is still worry, except now it generates documents, so it looks like work.

You cannot change the past. You can decide what the present pays to it.
