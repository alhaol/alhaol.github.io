"Open-weight" used to describe a file. Last week it started describing a plan.

Three open models were announced within two days. Reflection's Beam: weights promised later this month, access by waitlist. Mistral Large 4: API preview now, weights by the end of the month, license not yet named. Google's EmbeddingGemma 2: weights on Hugging Face, Apache 2.0, benchmark tables on the card, all on day one.

Same word, three very different amounts delivered behind it. Beam's benchmark tables are the vendor's own, and they were revised three days after launch. Mistral Large 4 can at least be measured by outsiders through its API, and Artificial Analysis already has, while labelling it proprietary until the file lands. EmbeddingGemma 2 can be downloaded, rerun on a public leaderboard, and tested on your own task today.

There are four rungs of evidence about any model: the vendor's numbers, an outside run, your own task, and a run where you control the weights and the license. Only the last rung is something a vendor cannot revise. A chart can be updated. A downloaded checkpoint is a fact.

None of this says which model is best. It says how much you can know right now, and a buyer can only act on that.

Keep "announced open" and "downloadable" as separate columns. The number on the card is not yet the number.
