A thousand years ago Ibn Sina wrote seven rules for telling whether a remedy actually works. All seven still catch bad AI claims.

He had our problem. People kept insisting something worked, and he had no way to tell a real effect from a good story.

So he wrote the tests. Test the thing itself, not the thing plus three other changes. Try it on one clear problem, not a tangle. If it seems to help everything, you have learned nothing. And make sure it happens again, because what happens by nature repeats and what happens by accident does not.

That is a clinical trial, seven hundred years before James Lind took oranges to sea.

Now hold it against the tool someone is pitching your team. A model that scores well on a public benchmark is, in Ibn Sina's terms, a drug that was tested on a lion.

If you keep one rule, keep the sixth. Ask how many times they ran it before the run they showed you.
