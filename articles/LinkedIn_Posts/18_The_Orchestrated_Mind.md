The smartest AI of the next few years will not be a single giant model. It will be a well-organized team of them.

In May 2026 something subtle changed in how the leading labs describe their products. Anthropic, OpenAI, and Google all stopped talking about one model answering one question. They started describing a coordinator that breaks a job into pieces and hands each piece to a specialist. Anthropic's finance agents have one agent pick comparable companies while another checks the valuation method. The frame moved from chatting with a model to running a team of them.

This mirrors the oldest idea in human organization: division of labor. A single large model holds the whole task in one place, works through it in one sequence, and has no second opinion. On long, complex work, that is fragile. Split the goal into focused sub-tasks, give each to a specialist, and add an agent whose only job is to check the others, and the whole becomes far more capable than any single part.

Here is the deeper point. We imagine general intelligence as one vast all-knowing mind. But the most capable human systems are not single people. A hospital, a law firm, a research lab: each is a general-purpose problem solver, and each is a coordinated group of specialists. Intelligence at the level that runs the world is already an orchestrated property.

For leaders, this is good news. The durable advantage is shifting away from owning the biggest model, which everyone can rent, toward designing the workflow that coordinates many agents around your specific problems. Value the orchestration layer. Map your work into roles. Always include a reviewer. Keep a human at the top.

The next race is not to build the largest mind. It is to build the best-organized team of minds.
