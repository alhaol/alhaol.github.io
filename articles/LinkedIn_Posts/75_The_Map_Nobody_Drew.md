Three expert surgeons teaching a procedure left out 71% of the knowledge steps. Your onboarding guide does the same.

That is not carelessness. Expertise hides its own steps, so the person who wrote your guide cannot see what it skips.

Picture three new hires on an AI team: a backend engineer, a statistician and a new graduate. By week two all three are stuck, two of them on the same page for different reasons. One has never thought about data leakage, the other never about base rates. The guide teaches neither, because its author handles both without thinking.

The fix is a written map of what depends on what. AI can draft it quickly, but do not trust it alone: in a 2026 study the best models also linked a quarter or more of topic pairs that had no link. So your senior engineer rules on every link, and new hires' answers keep or drop each one.

Then onboarding starts where each person is, not at page one.

Ask an AI assistant to draft a map of your team's core topics, and have your senior engineer accept or reject every link.
