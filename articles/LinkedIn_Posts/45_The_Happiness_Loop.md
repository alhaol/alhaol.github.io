In 1989 two researchers gave pagers to 78 working adults and beeped them at random for a week.

Most of their deeply absorbed moments came from work, not from free time. They reported them and still said they would rather have been somewhere else. We are bad at knowing where our own happiness comes from.

The good hours share four things. You chose the activity. It sits a little above what you can comfortably do. You know when you are finished. The work tells you the truth as you go.

Chosen. Stretched. Finishable. Honest.

None of those is a mood. They are structural, so they can be built. Run any task past those four words and the one it fails is the one to fix.

An AI can build all four, for any subject, at any hour. By default it does the opposite. It hands you the finished answer, which drops the challenge to zero, and it turns your work into checking someone else's. That is why a productive day with an assistant can still leave you flat.

So change the ask. Stop asking it to explain. Ask it for the next problem you can just barely solve, and tell it to hold the answer until you have tried.
