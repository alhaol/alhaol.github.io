Lesson 2 • Chapters 2, 3 & 4

The 3 Core Tax Containers: TFSA vs. RRSP vs. FHSA

In Chapter 2 (TFSA Explained) and Chapter 3 (RRSP Explained), each account is a container with different rules for deposits, growth, and withdrawals.

100% Tax-Free Forever $7,000/yr Room

1. TFSA (Ch. 2 & Ch. 9)

Funded with after-tax dollars. All capital gains, dividends, and withdrawals are 100% tax-free and never claw back government benefits. Room starts at age 18—so your 18-year-old child gets $7,000 room this year!

Best For: High-growth equity ETFs (XEQT, VFV)
Tax Refund + CCB Boost 18% Earned Inc.

2. RRSP & Spousal RRSP (Ch. 3–4)

Deducts against your top marginal tax rate today AND lowers Family Net Income to boost Canada Child Benefit (CCB) for your 15 & 12-year-olds. Use a Spousal RRSP so retirement withdrawals are taxed at your spouse's lower bracket!

Best For: High earners (>$106K) & US Dividend ETFs
RRSP + TFSA Hybrid $8K/yr • $40K Cap

3. FHSA (18yo Child Eligible!)

Tax-deductible going in AND 100% tax-free going out for a first home! Even if you already own your home, your 18-year-old child is a first-time buyer and can open an FHSA today to unlock $8,000/yr room (carrying the tax deduction forward to their career!).

Rule: Must OPEN account to start $8,000/yr room!

Which Investments Go Inside Which Container?

1. High-Growth All-In-One Equity ETFs (XEQT, VEQT, VFV) → TFSA & FHSA: Since TFSA withdrawals are 100% tax-free forever, put your highest long-term compounders here.
2. US-Listed Dividend Stocks & ETFs in USD (VTI, SCHD) → RRSP / Spousal RRSP: Under the Canada–US Tax Treaty, the RRSP is exempt from the 15% IRS dividend withholding tax. In a TFSA, RESP, or FHSA, that 15% US dividend tax is lost forever!
3. GICs, Bonds & Cash ETFs (CASH.TO, CBIL) → Kid #1 (18) & Kid #2 (15) RESP: Protect tuition money needed within 1–3 years from sudden stock market drops. Never hold GICs in a non-registered account (interest is taxed at 100% of your marginal rate).
4. Eligible Canadian Dividend Stocks (Banks, Utilities) → Non-Registered (Once TFSA/RRSP Full): Qualifies for the 15.02% Federal + 10% Ontario Dividend Tax Credit.
Ch. 2, 4 & 9 Warnings

Four Expensive CRA Traps to Avoid

1. CRA My Account TFSA Room Lag: Banks only report your prior year's TFSA activity to the CRA in late February. Relying on CRA My Account in January–April is the #1 cause of accidental over-contributions. Keep your own family tracker!
2. Form T1213 Paycheque Secret (Ch. 4): Don't wait until April for a $7,000 RRSP refund! File CRA Form T1213 so your employer reduces tax deducted at source on every paycheque all year.
3. Spousal RRSP 3-Calendar-Year Rule: If your lower-income spouse withdraws from a Spousal RRSP within the year of contribution or the 2 following calendar years, the withdrawal taxes back to the higher earner.
4. No Day-Trading in TFSA: Frequent speculative trading inside a TFSA can be audited by the CRA as a business, making 100% of gains taxable.

With children aged 18, 15, and 12, each child sits at a completely different stage of the CRA rules: Age 18 is in the $0-Tax EAP Withdrawal & TFSA/FHSA Launch stage, Age 15 is in the FINAL deadline year for the CRA Age-15 CESG Gate, and Age 12 is in the 6-Year Catch-Up Grant stage.

🚨 Urgent Dec 31 Deadline for Your 15-Year-Old

The CRA "Age 15 Rule" for 16 & 17-Year-Old RESP Grants

Under official ESDC / CRA rules, a 16 or 17-year-old is ONLY eligible for the 20% CESG grant if, before December 31 of the calendar year they turn 15, EITHER: (A) at least $2,000 total was contributed to (and not withdrawn from) their RESP, OR (B) at least $100/year was contributed in any 4 prior calendar years.
