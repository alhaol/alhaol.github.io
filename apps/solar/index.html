Reading the Sticker on the Back of Any Panel

1. Rated Watts (Pmax / STC): Tested in a lab at 25°C with bright artificial flashlights. In real outdoor sun, expect 75% to 85% of the sticker number (a 200W panel typically makes 150W–170W).
2. Voc (Open-Circuit Voltage) — THE SAFETY LIMIT: The maximum electrical pressure when plugged in before current flows. NEVER exceed your power station's max input voltage (e.g., 60V on EcoFlow DELTA 3), or you will permanently fry the charge controller!
3. Vmp & Imp (Operating Voltage & Amps): The voltage and current while actively charging. Power = Vmp × Imp (e.g., 20V × 10A = 200W).
4. Battery Charge Temperature (on the power station, not the panel): Most LFP batteries cannot charge below 0°C unless the station has self-heating. In a Canadian winter, keep the station indoors or in a heated space and run the solar cable outside to it.

Ottawa Winter Reality

Cold Panels Are Strong, Short Days Are Not

Sun hours: expect roughly 1.5 to 2 "peak sun hours" a day in December versus about 5 to 5.5 in June. A 400W setup that makes ~2kWh on a June day may make only ~0.6kWh in December.
Tilt steeper in winter: at Ottawa's ~45° latitude, tilt panels to about 60° from flat in winter (about 30° in summer). Steep panels also shed snow faster.
Snow: even a thin layer can drop output to near zero. Brush it off with a soft broom, never scrape the glass.
Safety & Code in Ontario

Three Rules Before You Plug In

Never backfeed your house: do not connect a power station to home wiring with a "suicide cord" or a dryer outlet. It can electrocute utility line workers. Whole-home or circuit backup needs a power inlet and transfer switch installed by a licensed electrician with an ESA permit.
Buy certified: look for CSA, cUL or cETL marks on power stations, chargers and smart plugs sold in Canada.
Fuse parallel strings: with 3 or more panels in parallel, put an inline MC4 fuse (about 15A) on each panel so one faulty panel can't be back-fed by the others.
Lesson 3 • Plugs & Wires

Solar Connectors & Interfaces Made Simple

Third-party solar panels almost always come with universal MC4 plugs, while power stations use specialized input ports. Knowing these 4 plugs unlocks any brand!

1. MC4 Waterproof

30A / IP67

The universal solar panel plug. Snap-locks together to keep rain and snow out. Safety Rule: Never unplug MC4s while bright sun is hitting the panel (prevents DC arcing)—cover the panel first!

2. XT60 & XT60i

EcoFlow / Anker

High-current yellow plug. Pro Secret: Always buy an XT60i cable (with the tiny middle sense pin shorted to negative). Without the "i" pin, EcoFlow limits charging to 8A (car mode) instead of full 15A solar speed!

3. DC7909 / DC8020

Jackery / Bluetti

Round 8mm barrel plugs used on Jackery and smaller Bluetti units. Note: Newer Jackery 1000 v2 / Plus models use the slightly thicker DC8020 pin (8.0mm × 2.0mm).

4. MC4 Y-Branch

Parallel Combiner

A 2-to-1 splitter pair (one for + wires, one for − wires) that combines two solar panels in Parallel so voltage stays low while Amps double!

Lesson 6 • Measure, Don't Guess

Devices to Measure Power, Voltage & Current

Every number on this page is an estimate until you measure it. Two meters cover the house side (AC), and four cover the solar side (DC). Start with #1 and #3: together they answer "what does it use?" and "is my wiring safe?"

AC • Plug-in

1. Plug-in Power Meter (e.g., P3 Kill A Watt EZ, P4460)

Measures: Watts, volts, amps and kWh of any 120V appliance plugged through it.

Use it to: learn what your fridge, TV or router really draws, so the sandbox and runtime math use your numbers instead of guesses.

What to look for: 15A / 1,800W rating, cumulative kWh, and a cost-per-kWh setting.

Search on Amazon.ca ↗
AC • Over time

2. Smart Plug with Energy Monitoring (e.g., TP-Link Kasa KP125M)

Measures: Live watts plus daily and monthly kWh, logged in a phone app.

Use it to: check which loads run during the 4 PM to 9 PM peak and how much your peak-shaving actually shifts.

What to look for: "energy monitoring" in the listing (many smart plugs do not measure), 15A rating, and cETL or CSA marks.

Search on Amazon.ca ↗
DC + AC • Essential

3. Digital Multimeter (CAT III, 600V)

Measures: DC and AC voltage, resistance and continuity; small DC currents on its 10A range.

Use it to: read a panel's open-circuit voltage (Voc) before you plug it in, and check series strings stay under your station's 60V limit.

What to look for: auto-ranging, CAT III 600V or better, and fused probes. Do not use its 10A range on panels that can push more current.

Search on Amazon.ca ↗
DC + AC • No wire cutting

4. AC/DC Clamp Meter

Measures: Current flowing through a wire, read by clamping around it, plus voltage.

Use it to: see the real amps from your panels on a cloudy versus sunny day without unplugging anything.

What to look for: "AC/DC current" in the specs. Many cheaper clamp meters measure AC current only and will read zero on solar cables.

Search on Amazon.ca ↗
DC • Inline

5. Inline DC Watt Meter (XT60 or Anderson)

Measures: Volts, amps, watts and total Wh flowing from the panel cable into the station.

Use it to: log a whole day of solar harvest and compare it with the station's own display.

What to look for: connectors that match your cable (XT60 for EcoFlow), a rating above your Voc and current (at least 60V and 20A), and thick 12AWG or 10AWG leads.

Search on Amazon.ca ↗
DC • Panel check

6. Solar Panel Tester (MPPT Tester)

Measures: A panel's Voc, short-circuit current (Isc) and maximum power point in full sun.

Use it to: confirm a new or used panel delivers close to its sticker watts before you build a system around it.

What to look for: a voltage and wattage range above your panel, MC4 leads, and an MPPT (max power) test mode.

Search on Amazon.ca ↗
Safety first: AC plug-in meters (#1, #2) are for 120V outlets only and cannot read solar DC. On the DC side, every meter must be rated above your panel's cold Voc. Measure Voc with nothing connected, cover panels before unplugging MC4s, and never measure inside your home's electrical panel. That is a job for a licensed electrician.
