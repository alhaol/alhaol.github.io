// the asymmetry your board needs to understand

The institutions with the most to lose are moving the fastest

  • Faculty productivity dashboards show throughput gains. They do not show fabricated citations, smoothed-over disconfirming evidence, or unverified synthesis.
  • A decade of AI-assisted shortcuts produces a decade of retraction-prone literature. The lag between adoption and reputational damage is structural.
  • Reputational capital is asymmetric: a single named retraction at a research-intensive university is a multi-year story. At a teaching-focused institution, it is a paragraph.
Bottom line: the universities racing fastest to capture productivity gains are the ones with the most reputational capital at stake.

// appendix · steelman shelf

Strongest counter-evidence (for honest Q&A)

  • Kestin et al., Harvard, Scientific Reports 2025 (N=194 RCT): AI tutor +0.73 to +1.3 SD vs. active learning. Caveat: durability beyond the session was not measured.
  • Wang & Zhang, Frontiers in Psychology 2026 (N=912, 3-wave): full-task delegation can outperform scattered offloading when AI is framed as a collaborator — a design variable, not a tool property.
  • Gerlich correction, Sept 2025: a table duplication was corrected; the r=−0.68 finding stands. Sample is UK convenience (N=666), self-report HCTA. Treat as direction, not magnitude.
  • OECD Digital Education Outlook 2026: intentional pedagogical purpose tracks sustained gains; general-purpose chatbots produce metacognitive laziness.
