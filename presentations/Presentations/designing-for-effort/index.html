// 01

What broke — the deliverable-only assessment

// the polish ceiling

AI raises the floor of fluency. Floor-calibrated rubrics stop discriminating.

  • The C-student now writes like a B+. The B+ student writes like an A−. The A student writes like the A− student. The top of the distribution collapses.
  • Rubrics that score grammar, structure, citation format, and surface coherence are now measuring the tool, not the learner.
  • Bastani et al., PNAS 2025 (N≈1,000): GPT Base raised practice scores +48% — and lowered unaided exam scores −17%. Same students. Same content. The deliverable lied.
The Bastani number is the single most useful thing in this talk. Repeat it.

// 02

What actually works — three patterns

// pattern 1 · oral defense

The 3-minute viva — substitution, not addition

How it works

  • Student submits the paper as normal
  • 3-minute structured viva on one randomly-drawn claim from their paper
  • Two questions: "Defend this claim" and "What would falsify it?"
  • Replaces the final-exam grading slot — does not add to it
Load math: 3 min × 80 students = 4 hours. Less than grading 80 finals.
+0.73
SD vs. written-only (verbal-exam pilot)
Lead with the load math. If you can't sell the time substitution, the rest of the pattern doesn't matter.
// pattern 2 · process portfolio

Grade the work of learning, not the artifact

  • Required deliverables: drafts (≥2), AI-use log with prompts and edits, 200-word reflection on what AI helped with and where it misled
  • Verification: one 10-minute mid-term checkpoint conversation. The log is verified by spot-check, not trusted blind.
  • What gets graded: reasoning quality, revision decisions, AI-disagreement instances. Not the final polished page.
  • Kofinas et al., BJET 2025 — process portfolios + prompt logs reduced undetected AI submissions in a UK business-school case.
Honesty mechanism: students fabricate logs only when nobody reads them. The 10-minute checkpoint is the cost of trust.
// pattern 3 · in-class synthesis

Frequent, low-stakes, closed-resource — the equity backstop

  • Format: 15-minute in-class synthesis, weekly or biweekly, handwritten or laptop-with-LockDown. Prompt is a transfer item from prior week's material.
  • What it measures: unaided cognitive transfer — the gap Bastani's PNAS study quantified at −17%.
  • Why it's the equity backstop: portfolios reward students with time to iterate. In-class synthesis rewards no one's schedule — only the learning.
  • Humanities variant: Socratic discussion grading or collaborative annotation — same principle, different surface.
Surface the equity argument explicitly. It pre-empts the strongest objection to portfolios.

Shift weight from polish to reasoning

Retrofit Old rubric Retrofitted rubric
AI-use disclosureImplicit / dishonor-codedRequired block at end of submission: what tool, what prompt, what edits
Reasoning ≥ artifact30% structure + 30% language + 40% argument20% structure + 10% language + 70% reasoning + sourcing

The shift: polish becomes a hygiene threshold, not a discriminator. Reasoning becomes the load-bearing scoring axis.
