// the playbook

Five moves · 18-month sequence · ~$340K incremental

  • 01 · Baselines — measure critical thinking, writing capacity, originality before deployment
  • 02 · Faculty development — funded and completed before tools land
  • 03 · Procurement — four contract clauses that protect academic function
  • 04 · Equity audit — three measured tests, ongoing
  • 05 · Outcome monitoring — 18-month loop against the baseline
Defensible mid-R1 incremental spend: faculty dev (~$180K) + governance (~$60K) + audit tooling (~$80K) + baselines (~$20K). Recoverable in Year 2 against avoided lock-in and Title VI exposure.
The price tag is the credibility move. Acknowledge it as defensible-estimate, not gospel.

// 02

Faculty development — before tools, not after

// the order matters

Trained faculty + AI = gain. Untrained faculty + AI = tax.

  • Funded: stipend or course-release. Unpaid PD does not happen. Plan for ~30 hours per participating faculty member in Year 1.
  • Before deployment: tools land in classrooms where rubric redesign has already happened. Otherwise you ship maximum disruption, minimum pedagogy.
  • Union dynamics: in 31 US states, faculty workload changes tied to AI are mandatory bargaining subjects. Engage the union before the pilot, not after the grievance.
  • UNESCO AI Competency Framework for Teachers (2024) — adopt the framework verbatim; do not invent your own.

// 03

Procurement — the four contract clauses

// real clause language

Four clauses that turn a procurement into architecture

ClauseSample language (defensible starting point)
Audit rights"Customer retains right to third-party algorithmic audit at vendor expense if disparate-impact threshold of 0.8 is breached on any protected class."
Data sovereignty"All student work and prompt data remain customer property; no use for model training without per-cohort opt-in; deletion on request within 30 days."
Model behavior"Vendor provides written behavior spec; material changes require 90 days notice and renegotiation; customer may suspend usage during transition."
Exit terms"Full export of student artifacts and configuration in non-proprietary format; 12-month transition assistance; no exit fee beyond pro-rated license."
EDUCAUSE 2025: 94% of IT involved in procurement, only 58% teaching-and-learning. That gap is what these clauses close.
// 04

Equity — audit as you go

// three measurable tests

Three equity audits, every term

  • Paid-tier vs. free-tier outcomes — measure assignment-level performance by reported tool access. Site licenses with paid-tier parity close this directly.
  • ESL student gain/loss — measure unaided writing trajectory for ESL cohort vs. domestic. ScienceDirect 2025 meta-analysis: AI produces large EFL gains when scaffolded; harms when unstructured.
  • Training-access gap — only ~36% of students report formal AI training. Measure it. Close it as a graduation requirement, not an extracurricular.
Counter to the "AI widens gaps" narrative: Stanford Tutor CoPilot showed +9pp gains for students of lowest-rated tutors. Structured deployment closes gaps. Default deployment widens them. The choice is yours.
