Cognitive load — instrument it, don't theorize about it

// from concept to metric

Lodge & Loble's distinction needs an operationalization

  • Beneficial offloading: free working memory for the intrinsic load — the hard thinking the learner is supposed to do.
  • Detrimental offloading: outsource the intrinsic load itself — student never engages.
  • The shipping question: how do you measure which kind your tool is producing?

// 03

Formative feedback over completion

// the completion trap

Hand the student the answer = the wrong metric won

  • What completion optimizes: session length, perceived helpfulness, NPS. None of these correlate with learning.
  • What learning optimizes: delayed retention, transfer to novel items, unaided performance.
  • The design move: withhold solutions; scaffold steps; require the learner to articulate reasoning before the tool advances.
Encoding into weights, not prompts: RLHF / DPO with educator-labeled preferences that rank scaffolded-incorrect above direct-correct. This is how LearnLM does it. This is the alignment work most teams skip.
// the steelman shelf

Where the "always withhold" rule breaks

  • Wang & Zhang, Frontiers in Psychology 2026 (N=912, 3-wave): full-task delegation can outperform scattered offloading — when AI is framed as a collaborator on a complex authentic task.
  • Stanford Tutor CoPilot (Wang & Demszky, 2024): AI supports the tutor, not the student. +9pp math mastery for students of lowest-rated tutors.
  • Implication for design: the unit of optimization matters. Don't optimize the learner-tool interaction in isolation. Sometimes the right intervention is upstream of the learner.
Give Wang & Zhang 90 seconds. It is the strongest counter to the deck's thesis.
// 04

Durability — the metric most teams aren't shipping

// concrete metric · concrete protocol

Normalized Learning Gain on a transfer item set

DimensionSpecification
ProbeTransfer items (not training items) — IRT-calibrated bank, held-out
Delay≥ 7 days post-session (Barcaui-style; not in-session)
ConditionAI disabled. No fallback. No exception.
MetricNLG = (post − pre) / (max − pre); report by item-difficulty stratum
Reporting cadenceEvery release; regression alerts ≥ 0.05 NLG drop
"Build for the test the learner takes alone." That's the metric. Repeat it.

// what to benchmark against

The evaluation harness most teams skip

  • IRT-calibrated item banks per subject — held-out probes that don't leak into training
  • Public benchmarks worth wiring up: LearningQ, MMLU-Ed, MATH (transfer subset), MathDial
  • Counterfactual cohort: active-learning baseline classroom, same content, same time horizon
  • Drift alarms: NLG regression ≥ 0.05; hint-rate spike ≥ 30%; self-explanation token drop ≥ 25%
Honest framing: if your team can't name the held-out probe set, you do not yet have a learning-engineering product. You have a chatbot with a school logo.
