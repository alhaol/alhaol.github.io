Nobody on your team has ever put an unimportant task at the top of their day. And yet most of last week went to work that will not matter by Friday.

That is not a discipline problem. Urgent and important feel like one signal, and they are two. Urgency is a clock, and it usually belongs to someone else. Importance has no clock, so it waits forever.

The part that costs you is what happens next. Work you skip because nothing is forcing it does not sit still. It ages until something breaks and hands it a deadline. Then the crisis it became eats exactly the hours you needed to prevent the next one.

The page walks you into that loop through one engineer who got praised in an all-hands for a 1 a.m. fix. The fire she put out was lit by the ticket she skipped nine weeks earlier. Then it hands you the boxes and makes you draw the arrows yourself.

Check your own calendar for one undated item you have moved three weeks running.
