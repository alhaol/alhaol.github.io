Every outage has something broken in it. That assumption is why 3am incident calls spend their first twenty minutes hunting a fault that is no longer there.

Some outages have a trigger that lasted twenty seconds and a loop that lasted three hours. Everyone looks for the trigger. The loop is what kept you up.

The shape: latency rises, so clients cross a timeout nobody moved. A timed-out client cannot tell a slow success from a lost request, so it retries, and that retry is a new request while the first still holds a server thread. One user action now costs three. The queue grows, everything behind it gets slower, and latency rises again. That closes a ring, and a ring does not need its entrance to keep turning.

The page walks you into the part that catches good engineers: at 03:14 you make the client more patient and more persistent, and the outage gets longer.

Then it makes you rebuild the ring and write why each arrow exists, because a postmortem room does not accept diagrams. It accepts reasons.
