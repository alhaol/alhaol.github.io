Think of the clearest thing you have ever written. The memo with no wasted sentence, the pitch where every number was right. Now ask what anyone actually did about it.

A director once wrote eight clean pages arguing to shut down a product line. They opened with a real customer call from March, specific and sharp, and it landed. The room agreed. Nobody argued. Six weeks later a colleague proposed reviving that same product line, and nobody at the table could remember the question had been settled. Including two people who had read the memo twice.

It was not too long. It was not unclear, and you can prove that, because nobody had a single question. The story at the front worked, which is what makes this hard to see.

Being understood and being remembered run on two different memory systems, and careful writing usually feeds only one. A hammock tied to one tree is a pile of rope on the ground.

New memory class on where the second knot goes. It is one paragraph long, and it is not a summary.
