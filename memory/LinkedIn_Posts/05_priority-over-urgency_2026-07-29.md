Your priority list reshuffles every Monday. That feels like being responsive.

A real priority order is dull. It is measured against a fixed objective, so it holds for weeks and you can answer it before anyone walks up to your desk. If yours moves weekly, the thing you have been calling prioritization is queue discipline, and other people are sorting the queue.

Both sorts are legitimate and you need both. Urgency orders the work inside what the objective already selected. It never selects. The failure is running the urgent sort and then reporting its output as though the objective sort had produced it.

The trap here is the one almost nobody catches in themselves. A director pings, you take the bug (which may well be correct), and by Thursday you have a sincere reason that is about customer trust rather than about who pinged you. Eleven times in a quarter, each one defensible. The page shows why that pattern is invisible from the inside, then gives you the one habit that makes it countable on a Friday.
