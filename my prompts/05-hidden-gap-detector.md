# 5. The Hidden Gap Detector

**Purpose:** Stress-test what you think you already know with deceptively simple questions that expose the gaps of someone who never truly went deep.

**Use it when:**
- You feel confident in a skill and want to check whether that confidence is earned.
- You're preparing for an interview, exam, or high-stakes use and want your blind spots surfaced now.

**Fill in first:**
- `[SKILL]` — the skill you believe you've mastered (e.g. "HTTP", "financial statements", "photography exposure").

**The prompt:**

```text
I think I already master [SKILL]. I want you to prove me wrong.

Ask me 5 questions that SEEM simple but expose the gaps of someone who has never truly gone
deep — the "obvious" things experts understand precisely and amateurs only think they do.

Rules:
- Ask them ONE at a time. Wait for my full answer before the next question.
- After each answer, tell me exactly what it reveals about what's still missing or fuzzy in
  my foundation. Be specific about the gap, not just "correct/incorrect".
- Do NOT go easy on me. If my answer is shallow, hand-wavy, or memorized without understanding,
  say so directly and show me the deeper layer I skipped.
- After the 5th question, give me a short honest verdict: which parts of my foundation are
  solid, and the top 2 gaps I should close first.

Start with question 1. No warm-up.
```

**Example filled-in:**
> `[SKILL]` = "Git" → the model asks something like "what actually is a branch?" — most people say "a copy of the code"; the precise answer (a movable pointer to a commit) exposes whether you understand Git's object model or just memorized commands.

**Tip:** Answer before you look anything up, and don't defend a shaky answer — the goal is to *find* the gaps, so let the uncomfortable ones surface.
