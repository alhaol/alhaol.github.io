# The Commute Problem

Three colleagues get the same 60 minute lecture and a 30 minute drive. Nobody can write notes.

Amina plays it at 1.5x and finishes two days early. Bilal pauses after every concept to say it back in his own words, and only gets through two thirds. Chen spends 60 seconds before pressing play deciding what she is about to learn and what she bets it will cover, then listens straight through like Amina.

A week later they all have to use the material in a meeting. Rank them.

Most people rank on the wrong axis. The interesting part is not who wins, it is that all three of them solved the same third of the problem and left the other two thirds untouched, and one fact about the material flips the order completely.

Notes are not magic. They are an offloading device, and there are exactly two other ways to get the same effect without a pen. This page walks Bloom's six levels in reverse to get you there: the ranking first, the reasoning underneath it, then back to the ranking.

Based on Justin Sung's video on learning deeply when you cannot write notes.
