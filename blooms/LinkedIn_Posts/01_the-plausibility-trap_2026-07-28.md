Two robotics vendors pitch you. One shows practice footage so good that 92% of reviewers cannot tell it from a real camera. The other shows ugly, jittery video and a completely different number: contact force within 8% of what a test rig measured.

The pretty one is cheaper, ready sooner, and forty times larger. Which do you fund?

Here is the fact that decides it. Friction has no look. Photograph a floor that was just mopped and one that is bone dry, and the two pictures can be identical. A model graded on whether its video looks real is therefore never punished for getting grip wrong. It climbs the hill you pointed it at.

And the error does not stay small. A one degree bias nobody could spot costs 9 mm on a half metre reach. Over a 30 metre aisle it costs half a metre, which is a different pallet.

New Bloom Class. It opens with that vendor choice, refuses to answer, then builds the pieces until you can settle it yourself.
